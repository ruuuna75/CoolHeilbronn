Proposed Solutions

Targeted measures for Heilbronn's urban heat hotspots, based on local temperature sensor data and international best practices. Each solution identifies the recommended intervention, its local relevance, and lessons from real-world case studies.

1. Prioritize Shade Trees

Where: Verified western hotspot (+1.2 to +1.5 °C)

The Heilbronn Strategy

Prioritize shade trees along sidewalks and waiting areas within the verified western heat hotspot. The objective is to improve perceived thermal comfort, measured using the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI), rather than focusing solely on air temperature.

To ensure long-term tree health, provide adequate underground rooting volume, engineered soil trenches, and moisture-retention systems. These measures help trees withstand drought stress and maintain their cooling benefits during heatwaves.

Real-World Case Study: Medellín, Colombia

Medellín's Green Corridors (Corredores Verdes) initiative planted thousands of trees along 30 major urban arteries. Reported city data indicated localized ambient temperature reductions of approximately 2 °C. The initiative emphasized shade at pedestrian level, supporting the goal of improving thermal comfort in Heilbronn.

Real-World Case Study: Zurich, Switzerland

Zurich's Fachplanung Hitzeminderung (Heat Mitigation Plan) highlights the importance of urban greenery in heat adaptation. Tree decline associated with restricted soil volume and drought stress illustrates why Heilbronn should invest in adequate underground soil infrastructure and moisture management before planting, rather than relying on small sidewalk cutouts.

2. Improve Access to Cooling

Where: Public squares, bus stops, pedestrian routes, and other heavily paved areas with high heat exposure

The Heilbronn Strategy

More than 36% of the recorded temperature readings exceed 30 °C at 14:00 and 18:00. Use these findings to guide the timing and placement of accessible cooling facilities, drinking-water fountains, shaded seating, and misting systems.

Prioritize vulnerable residents and people waiting for public transport. Operate water-based cooling systems during periods of peak heat exposure, while ensuring water hygiene, accessibility, and efficient water use.

Real-World Case Study: Vienna, Austria

Vienna's Kühloasen (Cooling Oases) provide places of relief during hot weather. Fine-mist sprays and other cooling measures in paved public spaces can inform Heilbronn's approach to targeted cooling. Operating systems during peak thermal hours can help balance cooling benefits, water hygiene, and operating costs.

Real-World Case Study: Barcelona, Spain

Barcelona's Climate Shelters network provides access to indoor and shaded public spaces during extreme heat. Its planning approach offers a model for improving access to cooling in Heilbronn, with a potential target of placing vulnerable residents within a five-minute walk of a suitable cooling space.

3. Incentivize Green Facades

Where: Dense urban areas, narrow street canyons, and buildings exposed to strong afternoon sunlight

The Heilbronn Strategy

The local dataset indicates that changing horizontal pavement surfaces produces minimal air-temperature cooling (approximately ±0.06 °C). Heilbronn should therefore complement ground-level interventions with vertical greening that addresses heat absorbed and stored by building walls.

Encourage building owners to install green facades through municipal subsidies, simplified approval procedures, and technical guidance. Plant foliage can shade exterior walls and reduce solar heat gain, potentially lowering heat transfer into narrow streets and improving local thermal comfort.

Real-World Case Study: Vienna, Austria

Vienna's 50 Grüne Häuser (50 Green Houses) initiative supports the greening of building facades. Its combination of municipal support and facade-greening measures provides a useful model for Heilbronn. Green facades can act as a thermal buffer, reducing the amount of solar energy absorbed by brick and concrete walls and potentially limiting heat release into surrounding streets.

4. Develop a Digital Cool Routing Network

Where: Citywide walking and cycling routes, particularly during the critical afternoon heat period

The Heilbronn Strategy

Integrate local sensor measurements, tree-canopy information, shade mapping, and pedestrian infrastructure into a public digital routing tool. The application could identify cooler routes and thermal safe-zones, helping pedestrians and cyclists avoid areas with high heat exposure.

Prioritize the critical period between 14:00 and 18:00, identified through the local temperature analysis. Time-dependent route recommendations could help older adults, vulnerable residents, and active commuters select paths with greater shade coverage and access to cooling facilities.

Real-World Case Study: Barcelona, Spain

Barcelona's Cool Walks concept illustrates how environmental data, tree-canopy mapping, and solar geometry can support route planning that prioritizes shade at different times of day. A comparable system in Heilbronn could make existing sensor data more useful to the public without requiring extensive new physical infrastructure.
