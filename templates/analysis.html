Possible Causes

1. Industrial Areas and Sealed Surfaces

Industrial areas and sealed surfaces store heat during the day and release it in the evening (Oke, 1982). The city’s climate analysis also classifies them as heat-burden zones.

2. Limited Cooling from Open Land

Open land cools quickly after sunset. Cold air does not reach deep into the city, so the cooling effect is mainly visible beyond about 3 km from the centre.

3. Limited Tree Canopy Coverage

Trees shade the ground and release moisture from their leaves (Bowler et al., 2010). Cooling becomes stronger at canopy coverage levels above about 40% (Ziter et al., 2019). Only 10 of the 1,045 cells had more than 25% cover.

4. Limitations of Inner-City Measurements

The inner city is not the hottest zone. It has the most street trees, with about 10% cover, and the bike data stops at 23:00 local time, before the heat island is usually at its strongest.
